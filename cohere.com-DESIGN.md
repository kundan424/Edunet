# Cohere — organic enterprise agency

## Overview

An enterprise technology style that pairs clear, almost conversational promises with tactile, otherworldly materials. Light white and warm stone canvases give way to forest-green and near-black chapters, with restrained coral-to-violet accents. Regular grotesk and geometric sans typography, small technical monospace labels, atmospheric photography and layered interface scenes keep a spacious site both human and capable.

### Essentials

- A short promise becomes a two-line typographic object: the default opening uses CohereText at 96px/1, weight 400 and -0.02em tracking, centered above an 18px support line.
- The opening delays the sales pitch in favor of a product stage: 160px top padding, 40px media gutters and a 1360×560px scene beginning below the headline; the navigation holds the immediate conversion action.
- Controls are soft capsules against hard technical subjects: primary actions use 9999px radii, 12px 24px padding, 18px text and a 2px transparent border; dark-on-light and white-on-dark variants reverse cleanly.
- Color lives in bounded worlds rather than everywhere: white and #f0eee9 carry the default reading areas; dark green or black product chapters and a full-width atmospheric conversion scene provide concentrated contrast. Coral, violet and blue usually occupy 2px rules or edges, not paragraph text.
- Interfaces float over physical-looking abstractions: stage a readable product panel over fibrous terrain, translucent ribbed glass or misty green light. Every new landing page needs an atmospheric product scene; without finished media, use one simplified interface over a textured contour field.
- Editorial proof has a cut-paper exit: warm-stone story cards combine 16px insets, rounded 22px tops, mono categories and a lower-right angled white notch containing an arrow. Preserve this silhouette for proof content.
- The ending is a designed destination: use an immersive roughly 600px-high artwork-backed call with a 60px centered line, followed by an expansive #17171c directory footer with four compact link columns.

## Typography

- **Unica77 Cohere Web** — Section headings, body, controls and editorial cards. Plainspoken neo-grotesk with open counters, regular rather than emphatic weight. If unavailable, use **Inter**. Use 400 for most text and 500 only for restrained emphasis. Font stack: "Unica77 Cohere Web", Inter, Arial, ui-sans-serif, system-ui, sans-serif.
- **CohereText** — Hero promises, large numeric proof and closing lines. Geometric sans with distinctive rounded construction and a less corporate display rhythm. If unavailable, use **Space Grotesk**. Use weight 400 and retain -0.02em tracking. Font stack: "CohereText", "Space Grotesk", Inter, ui-sans-serif, system-ui, sans-serif.
- **CohereMono** — Uppercase category labels, dates and chapter markers. Small, evenly spaced technical annotations rather than code-heavy decoration. If unavailable, use **IBM Plex Mono**. Font stack: "CohereMono", "IBM Plex Mono", ui-monospace, monospace.

## Surfaces and depth

### Surfaces

#### White canvas · level 0

**Use:** Default reading surface.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff; color: #212121`

**Observed:** Large uninterrupted areas surrounding media and headings.

**Transfer:** Let negative space carry the hierarchy before adding decoration.

#### Stone chapter · level 0

**Use:** Warm backing for a stack of product stories or technical editorial material.

**CSS:** `background: #f0eee9`

**Observed:** Full-width color field rather than a floating card.

**Transfer:** Group related chapters on one continuous warm surface.

#### Dark directory · level 0

**Use:** Footer and dark supporting sections.

**CSS:** `background: #17171c; color: #fafafa`

**Observed:** Flat near-black without raised-card shadows.

**Transfer:** Use subdued gray labels and simple white links.

#### Glass product panel · level 1

**Use:** Signature immersive feature chapter.

**CSS:** `background: #062c22; border: 1px solid #e5e7eb; border-radius: 20px 20px 0px 0px; padding: 48px 20px 56px 85px; overflow: hidden`

**Observed:** Artwork makes a deep translucent environment behind the product UI. Black and pale-white scenes alternate within the same stack; the final pale panel has a full 20px radius and warm outline.

**Transfer:** Place pale frosted or dark ribbed-glass artwork behind a small legible product scene, keeping the text side visually calm.

#### Gradient rule

**Use:** Active capability or question marker.

**CSS:** `background-image: linear-gradient(to right, #ff7759, #7670c5, #4c6ee6); background-size: 100% 2px; background-position: top; background-repeat: no-repeat; padding-top: 8px`

**Observed:** A narrow colored line marks the expanded item; the whole content block is not a gradient fill.

**Transfer:** Reuse for selection and emphasis across different types of content.

#### Lead form panel · level 1

**Only for:** lead-generation sections

**Use:** White form on a contrasting band.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff; border-radius: 22px; padding: 40px 48px`

**Observed:** A 675px-wide desktop panel on warm stone for industry pages or indigo for pricing. No elevation shadow.

**Transfer:** Keep the explanation outside the white panel; only controls and consent live inside.

#### Recognition square

**Only for:** research recognition

**Use:** Award or independent-validation callout.

**CSS:** `background: #cbefff; border-radius: 12px`

**Observed:** 440px square with tiny mono institution text at the top and a short title, support and link grouped near the bottom. A pale-green companion sits beside it.

**Transfer:** Use two restrained pastel blocks for credible external validation, not a rainbow of feature tiles.

## Imagery and graphics

### Organic interface stage

**Use:** Signature product visual in the opening and selected feature chapters.

**Observed:** A large white product window floats over a black-backed fibrous terrain in olive, pale gold and green. Fine vertical strands describe folded hills; the interface is the crisp foreground, not a decorative mock browser. It fills nearly the entire content width and continues beyond the first screen.

**Transfer:** Use a single believable screen for the new product. If finished renders are unavailable, combine a simplified real workflow panel with a finely textured contour landscape.

**Build:** Create an SVG terrain from 60–100 nested, slightly perturbed curved paths sweeping diagonally upward across the right two-thirds of a black field. Vary strokes among pale warm stone, olive and translucent green, overlay fine turbulence grain, and place the UI above it with broad dark breathing room around the edges. Keep the page text outside this scene.

### Translucent product materials

**Use:** Immersive feature panels.

**Observed:** Oversized rounded glass channels have violet-lit rims and thin parallel ribs against deep green-black. A pale counterpart has frosted translucent corners and blurred green vertical volumes. Small dark interface panels float in front, while text stays on the left.

**Transfer:** Keep the physical material as the environment and let a focused product detail be the object. Minimum acceptable version is a restrained translucent shape behind a real interface, not a generic gradient rectangle.

**Build:** Layer two oversized rounded SVG rectangles on the right half, one offset upward and one downward. Use transparent fills, multiple thin pale strokes and 8–16px spaced vertical ribs; add a narrow violet highlight and a heavily blurred green ellipse behind. For the light scene, reverse the field to white, soften edge strokes and add fine grain.

### Atmospheric closing field

**Use:** Required immersive invitation associated with the ending.

**Observed:** A full-width green fibrous vortex resembles wind, surf and mineral texture, with the short white invitation centered over it. The image occupies a 600px-high band and leads directly into the dark footer.

**Transfer:** Use organic texture to make the last invitation feel expansive. A procedural flow field is the minimum equivalent when photography or rendered material is unavailable.

**Build:** Use an SVG turbulence displacement map to bend densely spaced curved green and stone lines around an off-center focal point. Layer soft translucent green clouds, darken beneath the white headline and add fine grain; keep the central text zone lower contrast than the outer edges.

### Human and architectural photography

**Use:** Benefit campaigns, industry context and company content.

**Observed:** Cinematic natural light, often cool green surroundings or warm late-day buildings. A runner is paired with a grass-textured panel; office towers and city skylines provide industry scale. Photography is wide or square with restrained rounded framing, not a tiny decorative thumbnail.

**Transfer:** Choose a real setting where the product's benefit is felt; keep people candid and architecture monumental, with subdued contrast.

### Editorial proof thumbnails

**Use:** Story-card and news-card media.

**Observed:** Wide 16:9 images combine architecture, abstract glass, atmospheric texture or a customer mark. Warm stone surrounds the image; labels and titles sit below rather than over busy art.

**Transfer:** Use subject-specific media with one recognizable focus and the same restrained framing.

### Grained color sculpture

**Only for:** company storytelling

**Use:** Mission art and company closing invitation.

**Observed:** Floating soft triangular and pebble-like forms blend coral, purple and blue with grain, soft edges and dark cavities. The opening sculpture sits on warm stone; the closing artwork enlarges glossy dark forms over purple.

**Transfer:** Use a small family of physical-looking abstract shapes tied to the new identity rather than a mascot.

**Build:** Arrange four asymmetrical rounded triangular SVG paths at different rotations. Shade each using overlapping radial gradients in coral, violet and blue, add a dark translucent depression and overlay fine turbulence noise. Keep separation between forms and avoid hard vector-flat fills.

### Research halftone field

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Promise and organic product stage

**Observed:** White canvas behind a 48px announcement strip and 60px fixed Navigation. A centered two-line 96px display promise begins after 160px top padding; the 655px-wide support line follows by 16px. The heading occupies a compact central portion of the page rather than stretching edge to edge. After 80px bottom space, a 1360×560px Organic interface stage begins in 40px side gutters, with a dark organic field behind a large pale product window. Within the first 900px, the complete headline and support, the header's demo action and the upper part of the product object are visible; the scene's bottom and customer proof fall below the fold. There is no separate hero CTA under the default support line.

**Transfer:** Write a short two-part promise and stage one useful workflow over the procedural organic field. Industry pages instead use a left-aligned 60px promise with eyebrow, paragraph and dark CTA opposite a 616px-square architectural/product image. Pricing and company introductions use centered 72px type on cool gray or stone; research uses that centered type on deep forest, an inverse CTA and a narrower video below.

**Avoid:** Do not replace the first-screen stage with a small floating icon, force a hero button into the default text stack, or make the display bold.

**Text style:** `display` — CohereText 400 · 96px/1 · tracking -0.02em

### Heading and support pair

**Observed:** An uppercase mono eyebrow sits 12px above a regular 48px heading, with 16px to the 18px support paragraph. Some chapter introductions omit the eyebrow and center the heading within a 1128px-wide wrapper; resource headings are 32px with a browse link aligned right.

**Transfer:** Use category markers only when they provide orientation; reserve the larger heading for a new narrative chapter.

**Text style:** `heading` — Unica77 Cohere Web 400 · 48px/1.2 · tracking -0.01em

### Overlapping material chapters

**Observed:** On Stone chapter, 1360×650px Glass product panels overlap by 24px, with 20px rounded top corners. Text is inset 85px on the left, while the artwork and interface dominate the right. Chapter marker, 48px title, 16px description and capsule action form a consistent stack. The final pale chapter closes all four corners.

**Transfer:** Let a small set of major capabilities have different physical materials without changing their content architecture.

### Split capability selector

**Observed:** A large rounded product scene on the left faces a 555px-wide Capability accordion on the right. Selected content has a 2px Gradient rule, explanation and check-circle list; the other titles remain single ruled rows. Dark industry navy and research forest variants reverse the text and use white inactive rules.

**Transfer:** Give each selected workflow a different product detail while keeping image bounds stable.

**Only for:** research initiative pages

The following technical library changes to stone: open three-column principles, then 75px-high ruled paper rows with title left, topic pills near the right and date at the far edge.

**Transfer:** Separate quantified reach from technical substantiation; use actual metrics and real publications rather than decorative counters.

### Pricing comparison and questions

**Only for:** pricing pages

**Observed:** Centered 72px promise and paired actions lead into category tabs and equal 440px comparison cards on cool gray. A white section holds a 1130px-wide four-column rate table with a thin gradient header rule and restrained horizontal separators. FAQ layout places its 32px heading left and approximately 660px-wide accordion right, with 70px collapsed rows and one expanded response.

**Transfer:** Distinguish product selection, rate details and objections instead of compressing them into one giant pricing matrix.

### Company narrative panels

**Only for:** company pages

**Observed:** A stone opening pairs a short mission with Grained color sculpture. Chronological 1360×650px panels stack with 24px overlap, alternating blue-grained photography, lavender, pale ice and dark green. A spacious quotation leads into a dark portrait and executive-list chapter; square place photography and a candid recruiting split restore white space.

**Transfer:** Use chronology and people to explain institutional character without forcing company-only motifs onto ordinary product pages.

### Lead-generation conversion

**Only for:** sales-led pricing and industry pages

**Observed:** A two-column band places a 48px invitation, support paragraph and three checked benefits on the left and the Lead form panel on the right. The industry version uses warm stone; the pricing version uses deep indigo with white left-column text. Both finish at the shared Dark directory, without adding another immersive CTA.

**Transfer:** Use the form as the final action for high-intent pages, preserving the calm wide layout.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Promise → tangible product or contextual scene → capability chapters → credible proof → practical detail or objections → purposeful conversion → directory.

### Broad product and brand landing page

1. **promise** — Short centered display statement and organic interface stage.
2. **social proof** — Airy customer-logo strip and cinematic human-benefit band.
3. **capability chapters** — Overlapping material panels followed by a split use-case selector.
4. **validation** — Notched customer stories, open quotation, news and a dark certification chapter.
5. **closing call** — Green atmospheric invitation followed by the dark directory.

**Transfer:** Alternate concrete product usefulness with human context; use enough proof to support the promise without requiring every block.

### Industry-specific solution explanation and sales conversion

1. **contextual promise** — Split text and architectural product scene.
2. **capability chapters** — Navy open feature columns and workflow accordion.
3. **product detail** — Four pale model cards, demo video and compact resource links.
4. **validation** — Wide city image, security claims, diagonal quote-image diptych and editorial cards.
5. **closing call** — Stone explanation-and-form band, then dark directory.

**Transfer:** Translate capabilities into the buyer's workflows and constraints; finish with a specific expert conversation.

### Research initiative, model discovery and community credibility

1. **mission promise** — Forest centered display opening, inverse CTA and video.
2. **release discovery** — Split release announcement, four model variants and a model accordion.
3. **quantified proof** — Narrative beside large lime metrics.
4. **technical substantiation** — Stone principles and ruled publication library, followed by videos and pastel recognition squares.
5. **closing call** — Halftone-green invitation with primary and newsletter actions, then dark directory.

**Transfer:** Use the themed forest only for a sustained research world; balance accessibility with concrete technical detail.

### Commercial evaluation and buying-path clarification

1. **promise** — Cool-gray centered introduction, sales action and deployment link.
2. **selection** — Category tabs and restrained comparison cards with one gradient-edged emphasis.
3. **pricing detail** — Sparse rate table and explanatory split content.
4. **objection handling** — Long ruled FAQ beside a small heading.
5. **validation** — Diagonal testimonial carousel.
6. **closing call** — Indigo sales form and dark directory.

**Transfer:** Make product choice easy before exposing rate detail, then answer buying questions before asking for contact.

### Institutional story, people and recruiting

1. **purpose** — Stone display statement and colorful abstract sculpture alongside mission copy.
2. **history** — Overlapping chronological material panels.
3. **validation** — Large editorial quotation.
4. **people and presence** — Dark portraits and executive rows, then square city-photo carousel.
5. **participation** — Candid recruiting split and news cards.
6. **closing call** — Purple sculptural invitation and dark directory.

**Transfer:** Use genuine history and consistent photography to explain the organization rather than repeating product feature claims.

## Writing

Confident, useful and autonomy-oriented. Technical credibility is expressed through plain outcomes and explicit deployment or capability details, while human-benefit passages are warmer and more rhythmic.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Cohere names, logos, proprietary product or initiative names, customer marks, quotations, model specifications, security certifications, performance numbers or institutional claims. Replace them with verified content and assets belonging to the new product. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak directly to the reader with 'your' in promises and outcomes. Use 'our' or the product name for concrete capabilities, and reserve 'we' for institutional purpose.
- **Rhythm:** Short paired headline clauses contrast with one- or two-sentence support copy. Longer explanatory titles are allowed in specialist pages. Actions are brief verbs; avoid breathless punctuation.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hero promise | Two compact clauses give the reader ownership. | 4–7 words for the default landing promise | “Your AI. Your rules.” |
| Supporting line | Name the product class, then the reader's practical control or outcome. | One sentence, about 18–30 words | “North by [Brand] is a complete agentic enterprise AI platform that you control and deploy on your terms.” |
| Chapter heading | Pair capability with a business outcome; avoid a vague slogan when explaining a product. | 4–9 words | “Scalable, secure AI deployment”<br>“Secure, compliant, and ready for production” |
| Workflow title | Begin with an active verb and name the team or task. | 4–8 words | “Help investment teams move faster”<br>“Automate routine back-office work” |
| Primary action | Specific next step, not a playful imperative. | 2–4 words | “Request a demo”<br>“Contact sales” |
| Closing invitation | A brief question makes the broad promise actionable. | 5–9 words | “Ready to put AI to work?” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** your; control; purpose-built; secure; private; workflows; deploy; production; real-world; move faster

**Avoid:** magic; revolutionary as an unsupported adjective; effortless everything; game-changing; excessive acronyms; cute sales slang

**Rules:**

- Lead with what the reader can accomplish, then explain the mechanism.
- Use parallel verb phrases for capability sets and checked benefits.
- Tie security or efficiency assertions to specific product behavior.
- Keep mono eyebrows categorical and factual rather than turning them into extra slogans.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the default display at 96px/1 with weight 400 and -0.02em tracking.
- Use 40px desktop gutters and 80–144px chapter spacing around substantial media.
- Make the product scene broad enough to read as an environment, not a thumbnail.
- Retain 2px gradient accents as rules and emphasis edges.
- Use 22px editorial corners and the angled arrow foot for proof cards.
- Keep body copy at 18px/1.4, reducing to 16px/1.4 inside immersive panels.
- Let an immersive 60px closing line contrast with compact 14px directory links.
- Use a single focused interface detail inside each material chapter.

### Don't

- Do not use heavy bold display typography; the core headings are regular 400.
- Do not turn every button into a coral or purple filled badge; default actions are near-black or white capsules.
- Do not apply drop shadows to all cards; flat fills and faint outlines define their edges.
- Do not tint all reading sections green; neutral canvas provides the default rhythm.
- Do not round form fields into pills; they use 4px corners.
- Do not carry lime statistics or pastel award squares into ordinary product content without the research scope.
- Do not build a symmetrical centered footer; leave a broad brand-mark zone left of the link directory.
- Do not turn procedural texture into a busy layer directly behind paragraphs; product artwork and reading zones remain distinct.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Declared content entrances include fadeInUp at 0.5s ease-in-out and 0.8s ease, with some 0.1s or 0.2s delays; fadeInRight appears at 0.8s ease.
- Logo marquees declare 30s linear infinite motion.
- Buttons declare background-color 0.3s and color 0.3s transitions; many navigation color changes use 0.2s cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1).
- Navigation labels declare vertical word replacement on hover and focus, with 0.3s ease-out transform transitions; reduced-motion classes suppress these transitions.

**Transfer:**

- Use gentle content entrances to reveal the large compositional blocks, not constant movement of reading text.
- Keep any logo ticker slow and subordinate to the primary product scene.
- Preserve the impression of successive overlapping panels rather than unrelated card fly-ins.

**Unknown:**

- Precise scroll-pinning timing, full animated artwork behavior and every hover appearance are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Desktop navigation is fixed and shown at the large layout threshold; declarations include 768px and 1024px layout breakpoints.
- Class declarations reduce outer gutters to 16px, opening top spacing to 112px and bottom spacing to 48px in narrower layouts.
- Declared smaller type variants include 60px default hero type, 40px secondary hero type, 28px immersive-panel headings and 32px closing type; desktop cards switch from column layout to grid.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow screens stack text above the product material scene; preserve one coherent object rather than miniaturizing the whole desktop composite.
- Stack proof and comparison cards, keeping the notched feet and readable title sizes.
- Allow rate tables horizontal overflow rather than squeezing four pricing columns into unreadable cells.

**Unknown:**

- Exact mobile navigation behavior, all breakpoint mappings and mobile artwork framing are not visually established.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
